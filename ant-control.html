The first step is never the sprayer

Why identification comes first.

Ants are not one problem. They are many different problems wearing the same name.

Every ant species behaves differently — where it nests, what it eats, and how it responds to treatment. That is why the first step in ant control is identifying the species. Guessing wastes time, money, and can make things worse.

Take carpenter ants. If you treat them the way you treat fire ants, they can bud the colony — splitting into multiple new colonies — and your situation gets worse, not better. The wrong treatment can scatter the very problem you are trying to solve.

Inspection is the key. We look at the ants, their trails, their nesting signs, and the conditions around your home before we ever pick up a product.

Treating the wrong ant is worse than treating nothing. Identify first. Treat second. Follow up always.

Common in Northeast Alabama

Ants we treat in Scottsboro.

Each of these ants needs a different playbook. Here is how we handle the ones we see most.

ARGENTINE ANTS

The kitchen trail.

Those small ants trailing single-file across your kitchen counter are usually Argentine ants. They are some of the strongest trailing ants there are. They can sub-colonize in a potted plant or a stopped-up gutter, while the main colony stretches larger than a football field. Spraying the trail alone does nothing — we treat the trails and let the workers carry the product back to the colony, where it does the real work.

FIRE ANTS

Attack the queen.

Fire ants are a different game entirely. You have to attack the queen. We feed the workers, the workers feed the queen — and for the colony, lunch is their last meal. When the queen dies, the colony dies with her. That is why fire ant treatment is about the mound and the colony, not just the ants you see on top.

CARPENTER ANTS

Handle with care.

Carpenter ants nest in wood and can cause structural damage over time. Treating them like fire ants can bud the colony and make your situation worse. Carpenter ants need a careful, targeted approach — find the nest, treat it properly, and address the moisture that attracted them.

ODOROUS HOUSE ANTS

The smell test.

Often mistaken for Argentine ants, odorous house ants give off a rotten-coconut smell when crushed. They trail indoors for sweets and moisture and nest under stones, mulch, and siding. Identification separates them from the species that need a different treatment.

Why the technician matters. A well-trained, knowledgeable technician is one of the most important choices you will make. The difference between a spray-and-go visit and a real solution is someone who can identify the species, find the colony, and apply the right treatment — then come back and verify it worked.

Why they keep coming back

Why ants show up.
And how to stop it.

Ants follow food, water, and shelter. Change those conditions and you change the ant problem.

FOOD & TRAILS

Follow the trail.

Ants leave pheromone trails so the whole colony can follow. That is why treating the trail matters — and why simply wiping ants away never works. The trail stays, and the ants keep coming.

MOISTURE & SHELTER

Check the gutters.

Stopped-up gutters, potted plants, mulch against the foundation, and damp wood all give ants a place to nest. Argentine ants can sub-colonize in a single plant or gutter while the main colony lives far away.

THE COLONY

Size matters.

An Argentine ant colony can be larger than a football field, with the queen deep in the middle. Killing the ants you see does nothing if the colony keeps producing more. The treatment has to reach the colony.

THE QUEEN

Kill the source.

For fire ants, the queen is everything. Feed the workers, the workers feed the queen, and the colony is done. Species determines strategy — which is why identification always comes first.

Straight answers

Ant questions, answered.

Good service starts with a clear conversation — and continues with one.

Why do ants keep coming back no matter what I spray?

Because you are treating the ants you see, not the colony. Most ant species live in a colony that keeps producing workers. The treatment has to reach the colony — through the trails, the queen, or the nest — not just kill the ants on the surface.

Why is identifying the species so important?

Different ants need different treatments. Treating carpenter ants like fire ants can bud the colony, splitting it into multiple colonies and making your problem worse. Identifying the species first is the difference between solving the problem and multiplying it.

How long does ant treatment take to work?

Most ant treatments take 7 to 10 days to become fully effective. That is why follow-up is crucial — we come back, check the results, and re-treat where needed. One visit rarely finishes the job.

Are the treatments safe for kids and pets?

We place products strategically where pests travel and hide, rather than treating indiscriminately. Tell us about children, pets, aquariums, or sensitivities so we can discuss the appropriate precautions for your home.

Are ants covered in the general pest plan?

Common ants are included in our year-round general pest protection. Carpenter ants and other wood-destroying pests may require a specialized approach — we will let you know what your situation needs.

What can I do between visits?

Keep food sealed, wipe up trails (they carry pheromones), clear stopped-up gutters, and move mulch and firewood away from the foundation. We will point out the conditions that are attracting ants to your home.
