Common in Northeast Alabama

Mosquitoes we treat in Scottsboro.

Our warm, humid climate makes mosquito season long and intense. Here's what we're up against.

The most common mosquitoes in our area are the Asian tiger mosquito and the common house mosquito. Both breed in small amounts of standing water — a bottle cap, a clogged gutter, a flower pot saucer can produce dozens of mosquitoes. They are active from early spring through late fall, with peak pressure from May through September.

Beyond the itch, mosquitoes can carry diseases. In our area, that means concerns like West Nile virus and Eastern equine encephalitis. Reducing mosquito populations around your home lowers the risk for your family and pets.

You can't eliminate every mosquito in the neighborhood. But you can make your yard a place they don't want to be.

Why they keep coming back

Why mosquitoes show up.
And how to stop it.

Mosquitoes need standing water to breed and shade to rest. Change those conditions and you change the mosquito problem.

They're breeding. A female mosquito can lay 100–300 eggs at a time in as little as a bottle cap of water. Eliminating standing water is the single most effective step you can take. We help you find the sources during your inspection.

They're resting. During the heat of the day, mosquitoes rest in shaded, humid areas — tall grass, shrubs, under decks, and in trees. Treating these areas is where professional control makes the biggest difference.

They're attracted to people. Body heat, sweat, and the carbon dioxide we exhale all draw mosquitoes. While you can't stop being human, you can reduce the population around your home so fewer are drawn in.

Mosquito season in Northeast Alabama runs roughly from April through October, with peak pressure May–September. Treatments are most effective when started early and repeated through the season.

Straight answers

Mosquito questions, answered.

Good service starts with a clear conversation — and continues with one.

Is mosquito control included in the general pest plan?

No. Mosquito service is a seasonal add-on because it requires a different treatment approach and schedule. We can add it to your existing plan or provide it as a standalone service.

How often do you treat for mosquitoes?

During peak season (May–September), we typically treat every 3–4 weeks. Treatments break down over time, so repeating on schedule keeps the population down. We'll recommend the right schedule for your property after inspection.

Will I still see mosquitoes after treatment?

You may see some — mosquitoes fly in from neighboring properties. But you should notice a significant reduction. If activity is high between visits, call us and we'll respond.

Are the treatments safe for kids and pets?

We place products strategically in resting areas — vegetation, shaded spots, under decks — rather than broadcasting broadly. Tell us about children, pets, fish ponds, or sensitivities so we can discuss the appropriate precautions for your property.

When should mosquito treatment start?

Early. Starting treatments in spring before populations peak gives you better control through the season. But it's never too late — we can start mid-season and still make a noticeable difference.

What can I do myself to reduce mosquitoes?

Eliminate standing water — empty containers, clean gutters, fix drainage. Keep grass trimmed and shrubs pruned. Change birdbath water weekly. We'll give you a customized checklist after your inspection.
