That is why nest removal is part of our service. We treat the colony, confirm it's done, and take the nest down. No leftover invitation for next season's queens.

Common in Northeast Alabama

Wasps & hornets we treat in Scottsboro.

Each nest type needs a different playbook. Here is how we handle the ones we see most.

YELLOW JACKETS

The ground nest.

Yellow jackets are the #1 stinging pest in Alabama — and the source of most stings. Their nests are underground in old rodent burrows and lawn openings, so they're easy to step on without warning. We treat ground nests with insecticidal dust, which the wasps carry deep into the colony, then cover the entrance.

PAPER WASPS

The eaves nest.

Those open, umbrella-shaped nests under eaves, soffits, and porch ceilings are paper wasps — the most common nest we see on homes. They defend their nest, so we treat at dusk when the colony is home, then remove the nest.

BALD-FACED HORNETS

The aerial nest.

Large, gray, football-shaped nests high in trees and shrubs belong to bald-faced hornets. They are very defensive and will chase. Aerial nests get a long-range aerosol treatment at night, followed by removal when the colony is confirmed done.

EUROPEAN HORNETS

The wall void.

European hornets nest inside wall voids and hollow trees and are active at night, often attracted to lights. Wall voids get expanding foam treatment — and we never seal the entrance before treating, because trapped wasps will chew through drywall and end up inside your home.

MUD DAUBERS

Usually no treatment needed.

Mud daubers are solitary and rarely sting. Their mud tubes on walls and garages are mostly an eyesore. We'll tell you honestly when a nest doesn't need treatment — and remove the tubes when it makes sense.

CICADA KILLERS

Scary, but not aggressive.

Cicada killers dig burrows in bare soil and look intimidating, but they rarely sting people. They're often best left alone — we'll help you decide what your situation actually needs.

Identify before you treat. Ground nests, aerial nests, and wall voids all need different methods. Treating the wrong way — or at the wrong time of day — is how people get stung and nests get worse. That's why identification comes first, every time.

The work behind the results

How nest removal works.
Step by step.

Safe, thorough, and done at the right time of day.

01 / IDENTIFY THE NEST

Find out what you have.

Ground, aerial, or wall void — and which species. We locate the nest and plan the treatment before we touch anything.

02 / TREAT AT THE RIGHT TIME

Dusk or night.

We treat when the colony is home and wasps are sluggish. Daytime treatment scatters the colony and misses half of it — we don't work that way.

03 / APPLY THE RIGHT METHOD

Match the nest.

Aerosol jet for aerial nests. Insecticidal dust for ground nests — carried deep by the wasps themselves. Expanding foam for wall voids, with the entrance left open until the colony is done.

04 / REMOVE THE NEST

Take it down.

Once the colony is confirmed gone, we remove the nest so new queens don't reuse the spot. Reachable nests are included; ladder-accessed nests are quoted upfront.

05 / FOLLOW UP

Check for rebound.

Late-season queens can build replacement nests quickly. We follow up to confirm the area stays clear and catch any new activity early.

Never seal a wall-void entrance before treatment. Trapped wasps will chew through drywall to escape — and end up inside your home. We treat first, confirm the colony is done, then close the void.

Safety first, always

Why we take stings seriously.

A wasp nest is one of the few pest jobs where the danger is real — for you, your family, and us.

ALLERGIES

Tell us before we start.

Wasp stings can trigger severe allergic reactions. If anyone in your home has a known allergy, tell us when you call — it changes how we plan the job.

FALL RISK

The ladder is the real danger.

Most nest-related injuries aren't stings — they're falls. We never rush a nest, and we quote ladder-accessed nests clearly before we climb.

KIDS & PETS

Stay inside during treatment.

We'll ask you to keep people and pets indoors while we work. It takes a few minutes and keeps everyone out of the flight path.

GROUND NESTS

Easy to step on.

Yellow jacket ground nests are the reason most stings happen — someone mows or steps right over the entrance. We mark and treat them so your yard is safe again.

Straight answers

Wasp & hornet questions, answered.

Good service starts with a clear conversation — and continues with one.

Why do you treat at night?

Because that's when the colony is home and wasps are sluggish. Treating during the day scatters the colony, misses half the wasps, and puts everyone at risk of stings. Night treatment gets the whole colony in one visit.

Do you remove the nest?

Yes. Nest removal is part of our service. Treating a nest and leaving it invites new queens to move in, so we take the nest down after the colony is confirmed gone.

Will there be extra charges for a ladder?

Nests we can reach from the ground are removed as part of the service. If a nest requires a ladder to reach safely, an additional charge may apply — and we always quote it clearly before we climb.

Are wasps and hornets covered in the general pest plan?

Stinging insects like wasps and hornets are typically handled as a targeted service because each nest is a separate job. We'll let you know what your situation needs when we quote it.

What if someone in my home is allergic?

Tell us when you call. We'll plan the treatment around it — and we'll never ask you to get close to the nest. Your safety comes first.

How long until the nest is gone?

Most colonies are eliminated within a day or two of treatment. We confirm the nest is inactive, remove it, and follow up to make sure new queens haven't moved in.
