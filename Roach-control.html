Roaches we treat in Scottsboro.

Four species show up in our area — and each one is a different problem.

The German roach is the one that infests kitchens and bathrooms, reproduces fast, and is almost always brought in from somewhere else. The American roach is the large one that comes up through drains and sewers. The Smokey Brown roach flies in from trees and gutters, drawn to porch lights at night. And the Oriental roach prefers cool, damp places like crawl spaces and under sinks.

Because they live in different places and enter differently, one generic spray doesn't solve them all. We identify the species, treat where that species lives, and fix how it's getting in.

If you're seeing roaches in the daytime, the infestation is already serious. Roaches are nocturnal — daytime sightings mean the population has outgrown its hiding places.

The Custom Pest approach
01 / THE INFESTER

German roach

The most common indoor roach — small, light brown with two dark stripes behind the head. Lives in kitchens and bathrooms, breeds fast, and hitchhikes in on boxes, bags, and appliances. One egg case can produce 30–40 nymphs. This is the species that takes over a home.

02 / THE SEWER ROACH

American roach

The largest roach we see — reddish-brown, up to two inches, with a yellowish figure-eight pattern behind the head. Lives in sewers, drains, and basements, and can fly. Often called a palmetto bug. It enters through drains, pipes, and utility openings.

03 / THE FLYER

Smokey Brown roach

Uniformly dark brown to almost black, about an inch to an inch and a half. Lives outdoors in trees, gutters, eaves, and woodpiles — and flies to porch lights at night. Common in the South. It enters through attic vents, roof gaps, and open doors.

04 / THE MOISTURE LOVER

Oriental roach

Dark, shiny, almost black, about an inch long. Prefers cool, damp places — crawl spaces, basements, under sinks, and around drains and mulch. Slower-moving and doesn't fly. Sometimes called a water bug. Moisture is its magnet.

The work behind the results

How roach treatment works.
Step by step.

We don't just fog and hope. We identify, treat the source, and close the door.

01 / IDENTIFY & INSPECT

Know the enemy first.

We identify the species and inspect where it lives and travels — kitchens, bathrooms, drains, crawl spaces, attic vents, and the perimeter. We look for droppings, egg cases, smear marks, and daytime activity, and document what we find.

02 / TARGETED BAIT & TREATMENT

Treat where that species lives.

Gel baits, insect growth regulators, and crack & crevice treatments placed in the harborage areas of the specific species — not broadcast everywhere. Baits are slow-acting on purpose, so roaches carry them back and share them with the colony.

03 / FIND & SEAL ENTRY POINTS

Close the door behind them.

We trace how they're getting in — drains and pipes for American roaches, attic vents and roof gaps for Smokey Browns, door sweeps and utility openings for all of them. We point out what to seal and quote any work separately.

04 / MONITOR & FOLLOW UP

Keep the pressure on.

Sticky monitors track activity between visits, and follow-up treatments are included for covered pests. German roach infestations in particular need persistence — we stay on it until it's resolved.

Honest expectations. German roach infestations take time to eliminate — the bait has to work through the colony, and egg cases keep hatching for weeks. Why they keep coming back

Why roaches show up.
And how to stop it.

Roaches follow food, water, warmth, and darkness. Change those conditions and you change the roach problem.

They hitchhike. German roaches ride in on grocery bags, cardboard boxes, appliances, and secondhand furniture. They're the species you didn't invite — and the one that takes over fastest. Inspect boxes before they come inside.

They come up through drains and pipes. American and Oriental roaches live in sewers and drains and move through plumbing and utility openings. A drain treatment and simple sealing stops a surprising number of them.

They fly in from outside. Smokey Brown roaches live in trees, gutters, and eaves and are drawn to porch lights at night. Reducing outdoor lighting, sealing attic vents, and trimming branches away from the roof makes your home far less attractive.

Moisture is a magnet. Leaky pipes, standing water, damp crawl spaces, and mulch against the foundation draw roaches — especially Oriental roaches. We'll point out the moisture conditions that need attention.

Straight answers

Roach questions, answered.

Good service starts with a clear conversation — and continues with one.

Are roaches covered in the general pest plan?

Yes. Roaches are included in our year-round general pest protection. German roach infestations may require additional follow-up visits, which are included for covered pests until the problem is resolved.

Why do I keep seeing roaches even after spraying?

Because one generic spray doesn't address the species, the source, or the entry point. If you're treating but roaches keep coming, they're either breeding faster than you're killing them (German roaches) or entering from outside faster than you're treating (American, Smokey Brown, Oriental). That's why we identify first and treat the source.

I saw a roach in the daytime. Is that bad?

Usually, yes. Roaches are nocturnal — seeing them in daylight typically means the population has outgrown its hiding places. That's a sign to get an inspection sooner rather than later, especially with German roaches, which multiply quickly.

Are the treatments safe for kids and pets?

We use targeted gel baits and crack & crevice applications placed where roaches live and travel — not broadcast fogging through your living space. Tell us about children, pets, aquariums, or sensitivities so we can discuss the appropriate precautions for your home.

How do I tell which roach I have?

Size and color are the quickest clues: small and tan with two dark stripes is German; large and reddish-brown is American; uniformly dark brown and flying to lights is Smokey Brown; dark, shiny, and slow in damp areas is Oriental. We'll confirm the identification during your inspection.

Will roaches come back after treatment?

German roaches can be reintroduced at any time by bringing in infested items — that's why we also talk about prevention. American and Smokey Brown roaches can return if entry points stay open. We seal what we can, tell you what needs a separate quote, and your year-round plan keeps the pressure on.
