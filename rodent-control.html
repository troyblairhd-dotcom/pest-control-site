Common in Northeast Alabama

Rodents we treat in Scottsboro.

Two species cause most of the problems in our area — and they're not the same animal.

House mice are the most common rodent problem in Scottsboro homes. They're small — fitting through a gap the width of a pencil — and they reproduce fast. A single pair can produce dozens of offspring in months. They nest in walls, attics, and behind appliances, and they chew on wiring, insulation, and food packaging.

Norway rats and roof rats are less common but more destructive. They're larger, more aggressive chewers, and can gnaw through plastic, wood, and even soft metal. Roof rats prefer attics and upper areas; Norway rats tend to stay low — basements, crawl spaces, and ground-level openings.

If you're seeing droppings, hearing scratching in the walls, or finding chewed packaging, you already have a problem. The question is how many — and how they got in.

Why they keep coming back

Why rodents show up.
And how to stop it.

Rodents enter homes for food, warmth, and shelter — especially as temperatures drop in fall and winter.

They're looking for food. Rodents eat almost anything — pantry food, pet food, bird seed, even garbage. Storing food in sealed containers and keeping surfaces clean removes the food source. But if they're already inside, trapping comes first.

They're looking for warmth. As temperatures drop in fall, mice and rats move indoors. Attics, crawl spaces, and wall voids offer shelter and warmth. This is why rodent problems spike from October through March in our area.

They're looking for entry points. A mouse can fit through a gap the width of a pencil. Rats need only a half-inch opening. Common entry points include utility penetrations, foundation cracks, roof gaps, damaged vents, and gaps under doors. Sealing these is the key to lasting control.

Your year-round general pest plan helps monitor for rodent activity between seasons. If you're seeing signs of rodents, call us — we'll inspect and recommend the right approach.

Straight answers

Rodent questions, answered.

Good service starts with a clear conversation — and continues with one.

Is rodent control included in the general pest plan?

Trapping is a specialized add-on because it requires a different approach, tools, and follow-up schedule. Your general pest plan includes monitoring for rodent activity, but active trapping and exclusion are quoted separately.

How long does it take to get rid of rodents?

It depends on the severity. A minor mouse problem may be resolved in 1–2 visits. A larger infestation can take several weeks of trapping and follow-up. We'll give you a realistic timeline after your inspection.

Do you use poison or traps?

We primarily use traps for indoor rodent control. We can discuss bait stations for exterior rodent pressure, but we generally don't recommend poison indoors — rodents can die in walls and create odor and health issues. We'll recommend the right approach for your situation.

What is exclusion and why is it important?

Exclusion means sealing the entry points rodents use to get inside. Without it, new rodents will eventually find their way back in. It's the single most important step for lasting rodent control. We inspect for entry points and provide a separate quote for sealing work.

Are the treatments safe for kids and pets?

Traps are placed in areas where rodents travel — often behind appliances, in attics, or in crawl spaces — to limit access by children and pets. We discuss placement and any precautions based on your home and household.

What are the signs of a rodent problem?

Droppings (small, dark pellets), gnaw marks on food packaging or wiring, scratching or scurrying sounds in walls or ceilings, rub trails along baseboards, and nests made of shredded paper or insulation. If you see any of these, call us for an inspection.
